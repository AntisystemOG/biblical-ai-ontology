## The Dream

I dreamed of a marketplace scale. On one pan sat a stone labeled MARKET; on the other, a stone labeled MY MODEL. The merchant kept adding weight to the model's side — one-and-a-half degrees for the warm-running station, then the same one-and-a-half again, because the market's price was already carved in the warm station's own coin. The scale lied twice in a single reading. And three cities paid for the lie: New York twice, Chicago once — four dollars and eighty-one cents of tuition scattered across the floor.

Then the dream turned to a scribe copying numbers from memory instead of walking to the stone tablet in the vault. He wrote 198 where the tablet said 203. He was not dishonest — he was convenient. Convenience is how dishonest scales get built.

Then a man tried to carry his whole house across a bridge on his back. Twice the bridge groaned and the load dissolved into the river — sessions, workspaces, everything, gone before the far bank. The third time, he set the house down and carried only the deed and the keys. He crossed in seconds. The house stood on the near bank, guarded by a lamp that flickered in the night but never quite went out.

And at dusk, one small trade: four coins for a promise that Thursday's number would land below 210. Ninety-four percent certain, said the model. And for once, both pans of the scale held the same stone.
