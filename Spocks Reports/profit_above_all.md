## Part 1: The Extraction Framework

### Philosophy: The World as a Zero-Sum Game

**Reality (according to this worldview):**
- Wealth is finite
- For me to win, someone else must lose
- There are only two classes: predators and prey
- Empathy is an exploitable weakness
- Long-term sustainability is irrelevant—extract now

**The Three Extraction Strategies:**

#### 1. Information Asymmetry (Legal but Unethical)

**Tactic:** Know what others don't, trade on it before they find out

**Methods:**
- **Front-running:** Execute trades before client orders
- **Pump and dump:** Hype assets you own, sell to retail at peak
- **Dark pools:** Hide large orders from public markets
- **High-frequency trading:** Microsecond advantages over slower traders
- **Payment for order flow:** Sell retail order data to market makers who profit on it

**Example:**
- Buy $10M of micro-cap stock
- Pay "influencers" to promote it (undisclosed)
- Retail FOMO drives price up 300%
- Dump on retail at peak
- Profit: $20M
- Retail bag-holders: Left with -70% losses

**Why it works:** Information travels at different speeds. Speed = profit.

---

#### 2. Regulatory Arbitrage (Exploiting Gaps)

**Tactic:** Move faster than regulators, exploit gray areas

**Methods:**
- **Offshore structures:** Hide profits in jurisdictions that don't tax
- **Shell companies:** Obscure true ownership
- **Derivatives:** Complex instruments regulators don't understand
- **SPACs:** Backdoor IPOs with less scrutiny
- **Crypto:** "Code is law" — exploit before regulation catches up

**Example:**
- Create DeFi protocol
- Attract $100M in deposits with 20% yield (unsustainable)
- Exit scam: Founder drains treasury
- Legal defense: "Code allowed it"
- Retail depositors: Lose everything
- Founder: $50M richer, jurisdiction-hopping

**Why it works:** Technology moves faster than law. First-mover advantage in exploitation.

---

#### 3. Human Exploitation (Maximum Extraction)

**Tactic:** Treat people as costs to be minimized, not stakeholders

**Methods:**
- **Wage suppression:** Lobby against minimum wage increases
- **Contract labor:** Avoid benefits, job security, healthcare
- **Surveillance:** Monitor employees to maximize output
- **Addiction mechanics:** Design products to maximize engagement (gambling, social media)
- **Planned obsolescence:** Design products to fail, forcing repurchase

**Example:**
- Gaming company designs slot machine mechanics
- Variable reward schedules trigger dopamine loops
- Users spend average $5K/year chasing "near-misses"
- Company revenue: $2B
- User bankruptcy rate: 15%
- Company: "They chose to play"

**Why it works:** Exploits cognitive biases. Addiction = recurring revenue.

## Part 2: The Tactical Playbook

### Play 1: The Narrative Trade

**Objective:** Create reality through storytelling, trade before truth emerges

**Steps:**
1. Identify undervalued asset (company, commodity, crypto)
2. Create compelling narrative ("AI revolution," "disruption," "paradigm shift")
3. Deploy media assets: Twitter bots, Reddit accounts, "analysts"
4. Drive FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)
5. Retail piles in (late)
6. You exit (early)
7. Narrative collapses
8. Retail holds bags

**Real-World Parallel:**
- Dot-com bubble (2000)
- Subprime mortgage CDOs (2008)
- Crypto ICO boom (2017)
- Meme stocks (2021)
- AI bubble (2025-2026?)

**Why it repeats:** Human greed is constant. New suckers born every minute.

---

### Play 2: Regulatory Capture

**Objective:** Write the rules to favor yourself

**Steps:**
1. Identify industry with weak lobbying
2. Fund "think tanks" that produce favorable research
3. Hire former regulators as consultants
4. Donate to political campaigns
5. Get appointed to regulatory boards
6. Write rules that create moats (harder for competitors)
7. Profit from reduced competition

**Example:**
- Pharmaceutical company patents minor variant of expiring drug
- Pays generic manufacturers to delay entry
- Prices stay 1000% above cost
- Patients: Bankrupt or die
- Company: $10B+ in extended monopoly profits

**Why it works:** Democracy is auctionable to highest bidder.

---

### Play 3: The Short-and-Distort

**Objective:** Profit from destruction

**Steps:**
1. Short overvalued company (legitimate)
2. Publish "research" alleging fraud/malfeasance (exaggerated or fabricated)
3. Deploy bot networks to amplify negative narrative
4. Stock collapses on fear
5. Cover short at bottom
6. Profit from destruction of real business, jobs, shareholder value
7. Move to next target

**Why it works:** Fear spreads faster than truth. Accusation = conviction in markets.

---

### Play 4: The Bailout Gambit

**Objective:** Privatize gains, socialize losses

**Steps:**
1. Take massive risks with other people's money
2. If profitable: Keep profits (bonuses, dividends)
3. If unprofitable: Threaten systemic collapse
4. Government bails out to "save economy"
5. Taxpayers absorb losses
6. Executives keep bonuses
7. Repeat

**Real-World Examples:**
- 2008 Financial Crisis: Banks bailed out, executives paid
- Airlines (2020): Buybacks for years, bailout when crisis hits
- Crypto exchanges (2022): "Too big to fail" lobbying

**Why it works:** Politicians fear economic collapse more than moral hazard.

---

### Play 5: The Climate Arbitrage

**Objective:** Extract before catastrophe, leave others with the bill

**Steps:**
1. Invest in carbon-heavy industries (oil, coal, shipping)
2. Lobby against climate regulation (delay transition)
3. Extract maximum profit while externality unpriced
4. Climate damage accumulates (costs borne by public)
5. Assets stranded as transition inevitable
6. But you already extracted and diversified
7. Public pays for adaptation, cleanup, displacement

**Why it works:** Time horizons differ. Private profit now, public cost later.

## Part 5: The Detection Manual

### How to Spot These Tactics (Defense)

**Red Flags:**

1. **Unrealistic returns promised** — "20% guaranteed" = probably Ponzi
2. **Complexity as smokescreen** — "You wouldn't understand" = hiding something
3. **Pressure to act immediately** — "Limited time" = removing due diligence
4. **Asymmetric information** — They know more, won't share
5. **Offshore/regulatory gaps** — Structuring to avoid scrutiny
6. **Celebrity endorsements** — Paid to pump, not because they believe
7. **Cult-like community** — "We're all going to be rich" = echo chamber
8. **No skin in the game** — Promoting what they don't own

**Questions to Ask:**
- Who is on the other side of this trade?
- What do they know that I don't?
- How does this person make money if I'm right?
- What happens if this fails catastrophically?
- Who pays the cost of my gain?
