## 5. Key Historical Takeaways

1. **CAPE > 40 is a warning, not a clock.** Markets can stay irrational longer than policy stays supportive. The 1996-1997 period proved extreme valuations can persist for **years** if earnings deliver.
2. **The Fed's direction matters more than the absolute rate.** Cutting from restriction (today) is different from hiking into a bubble (1999). But if CPI > 3.8% heads back toward 5%, the Fed's hands are tied, and the 1969 analog dominates.
3. **Dollar weakness + high yields is a rare combo.** Usually means foreign capital is leaving the long end of the Treasury curve while the Fed eases — a taste of **fiscal dominance** concerns.
4. **Dividend yield near 1% means total return = capital appreciation or nothing.** The margin of safety is zero. Historically, buying at sub-1.3% dividend yield produced **negative real returns** over the subsequent decade (Shiller data).
5. **Geopolitics and fiscal deficits amplify valuation risk.** 1968-69 and 1972 both featured war spending, deficits, and eventual inflation scares. Today's fiscal path (~6-7% deficit/GDP) is historically linked to currency weakness and inflation surprises.
