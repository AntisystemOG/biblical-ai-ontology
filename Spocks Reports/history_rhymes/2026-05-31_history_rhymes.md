- Market became obsessed with a small group of "Nifty Fifty" growth stocks (Xerox, IBM, Polaroid, Avon, etc.) that were deemed "one-decision" buys at any price.

- Vietnam War spending + Great Society fiscal expansion created demand-side inflation pressure.
- Oil supply constraints were emerging (prelude to 1973).
- Sector rotation began as the market peaked.

**Outcome:**
- S&P 500 fell ~37% from 1968 peak to 1970 trough.
- The Nifty Fifty eventually collapsed; many names never recovered to their 1968–1972 highs.

## Part 3: Pattern Analysis — What History Teaches

### 1. Inflation + War + Fed Behind the Curve = Bad for High Valuations
Every historical instance where inflation re-accelerated during an ongoing war (1968–1970, 1973–1974, 1979–1980) saw significant multiple compression in the most popular stocks. The Fed's current posture—holding rates steady with dissent rather than acting preemptively—matches the slow-to-act pattern of the late 1960s Fed.

### 2. Extreme Concentration Is a Late-Cycle Phenomenon
The Nifty Fifty (1972), the dot-com five (2000), and the Mag 7 (2026) all represented peak market concentration. In each case, the narrative was that "these companies are different." In each case, the unwind was painful. History does not suggest concentration persists indefinitely.

### 3. Sector Rotation Is Often the Canary in the Coal Mine
When money rotates out of the leaders that drove the bull market (Tech → Industrials/Defensives/Energy), it historically signals either:
- A healthy broadening of the rally (best case), or
- A narrowing market where only a few names are working, followed by a top (worst case).

Given the extreme starting concentration in 2026, rotation is more likely to be a warning than a healthy sign.

### 4. Oil Shocks Have Predictable Effects
Every major oil shock (1973, 1979, 1990, 2008, 2026) has caused:
- A temporary inflation spike.
- Consumer sentiment collapse.
- Pressure on discretionary spending.
- Increased recession probability within 6–12 months.

The 2026 Iran war shock is still unfolding. If oil prices remain elevated or spike further, recession odds rise sharply.
