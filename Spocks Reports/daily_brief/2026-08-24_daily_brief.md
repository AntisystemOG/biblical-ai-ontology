## Key Stories

### 1. Treasury Debt Buybacks Ease Bond-Market Pressure
**Sources:** AP (Center) | Reuters (Center) | Axios (Center)

**WHERE THEY AGREE:**
- The Treasury Department intervened in the bond market by buying back longer-term debt.
- Global yields fell after the U.S. boosted buybacks.
- The move effectively swaps long-duration debt for shorter-term bills, lowering pressure on long-term rates.

**WHERE THEY DIFFER:**
- AP/Center reports the intervention as a technical relief valve; Axios frames it as potentially growing larger.
- Left sources (CNN) mention bond yields as a source of economic pain but spend more airtime on tariffs.
- Right sources focus on the Iran / sanctions angle as the real driver of risk, not bond mechanics.

**Blindspots:**
- Left: omits how unusual / large the Treasury buyback is and what it signals about debt-management stress.
- Right: omits that the buyback is the proximate reason yields paused, not Iran headlines alone.
- Center: omits the political optics of the Treasury engineering lower rates ahead of an election-sensitive economy.

**Likely Reality:** This was a deliberate policy maneuver to cap long-term rates. It worked for now, but it does not fix the underlying fiscal picture. It is bullish short-term for rate-sensitive equities (tech, growth, crypto miners), neutral-to-bearish long-term if it signals debt-sustainability concerns.
